Care standards

A clear room makes gentle care stronger.

Rose Quartz Massage should feel warm, intuitive, and human, but the care model also needs firm boundaries. These standards help guests understand consent, privacy, medical scope, and when provider guidance should come before booking.

Rose Quartz promise Light-touch care with clear consent, careful intake, and no pressure to push through pain.

Guests can ask for less pressure, a pause, silence, more guidance, a different position, or a skipped body area at any time.

Scope of care

Massage is supportive care, not medical diagnosis.

Rose can support comfort, relaxation, body awareness, pressure-sensitive therapeutic work, prenatal positioning, lymphatic-style flow, and nervous-system downshifting. She does not diagnose, prescribe, treat medical conditions, or replace care from a licensed medical provider.

  • New, severe, worsening, unexplained, or alarming symptoms should be evaluated medically.
  • Guests should share relevant provider guidance before the appointment.
  • Rose can adjust the plan, postpone care, or recommend medical clearance when needed.
Session language
Support, comfort, relief, relaxation, and care planning.
Avoid
Diagnosis, cure claims, medical promises, or pressure-as-proof messaging.
Best signal
The body receives the work without bracing against it.

Prenatal and postpartum care

Pregnancy comfort starts with provider guidance and positioning.

Prenatal sessions should use side-lying support, slow transitions, clear bolstering, conservative pressure, and frequent comfort checks. Postpartum sessions should respect healing, fatigue, feeding posture, emotional bandwidth, and any medical restrictions.

  • Pregnant guests should share trimester, provider guidance, high-risk notes, and positioning needs.
  • Unusual swelling, bleeding, dizziness, severe headache, blood-pressure concerns, fever, or new severe pain should be discussed with a clinician before massage.
  • Postpartum guests should share birth timing, incision or tear sensitivity, restrictions, and current symptoms.
Positioning
Side-lying, bolstered, adjustable, and never rushed.
Pressure
Light to medium only by consent and clinical context.
Best fit
Hips, low back, feet, neck, jaw, sleep posture, and quiet reset.

Lymphatic and swelling cautions

Gentle flow should never override medical red flags.

Lymphatic-style massage should be light, rhythmic, realistic, and safety-aware. It can support comfort and heaviness, but it should not be positioned as a cure or a substitute for medical care when swelling could signal a more serious concern.

  • Sudden, one-sided, hot, painful, severe, or unexplained swelling needs medical guidance.
  • Recent surgery, open wounds, infection signs, clotting concerns, cancer-treatment history, heart concerns, or kidney concerns should be shared before booking.
  • Travel-related heaviness should be screened for pain, heat, redness, shortness of breath, or other urgent symptoms.

Light, slow, and conservative.
Good fit

Symptoms are sudden, painful, one-sided, hot, severe, or unexplained.

Privacy and intake

The body map should collect only what Rose needs.

The Rose Session Map is designed to help guests communicate body areas, sensations, pressure preferences, room tone, and relevant cautions. It should not collect unnecessary personal history, and returning-client memory should only be added later with explicit consent.

  • Guests can share practical care details without explaining private stories.
  • Sensitive medical details should be handled carefully and only when relevant to the session.
  • Future AI intake features should draft summaries for Rose to review, not make medical decisions.
Collect
Body areas, pressure, tone, symptoms relevant to fit, and provider guidance.
Avoid
Unneeded personal history, ad tracking on intake, and saved preferences without consent.
Future AI
Assistant for summary and article matching, never diagnosis.
