# Competitive research notes

Last refreshed: May 20, 2026.

This build turns competitor research into product decisions rather than copying competitor layouts. The public site should not sound like a comparison page; the research should show up as a better booking experience, a more memorable care method, and stronger trust signals.

## Las Vegas patterns

- Local prenatal competitors make pregnancy massage easy to find and book. Massage Well has a dedicated prenatal page with direct booking calls-to-action, common discomforts, pregnancy benefits, and a doctor-consult reminder. IMR lists a 13-week minimum, sideline/pregnancy-pillow positioning, prices, durations, and direct appointment links.
- Premium Las Vegas spa competitors win with breadth, price clarity, and facility confidence. Canyon Ranch lists a large massage catalog and a prenatal massage with 50/80-minute options and premium pricing.
- Specialist Las Vegas competitors win with clinical confidence. N Balance frames initial MLD and medical massage around assessment time. Therapie positions itself around manual lymphatic drainage, post-surgical/breast/chest care, scar tissue, mobility, and a named method.
- Most local massage sites still ask guests to choose a modality first. Rose Quartz can do better by translating what the guest feels into a care pathway through a body map, pressure consent, room tone, and aftercare plan.

## Global leader patterns

- Six Senses wins through personalization, screening, ritual, local inspiration, and follow-up. Their wellness pages emphasize programs designed around inner health, movement, sleep, lifestyle discussion, and bespoke recommendations.
- Six Senses massage pages also show how to make a treatment feel signature: adaptive massage, a before-and-after ritual, local ingredients, and a layered wellness experience.
- Aman wins with journeys rather than isolated services: grounding, purification, water ritual, hydrotherapy, seasonal ingredients, privacy, and full-body/mind/spirit framing.
- Global luxury brands consistently make the arrival, treatment, and return feel connected. Rose can bring that thinking to an independent practice through a lighter, more personal digital experience.

## Product standards for Rose Quartz

- Lead with a sensory sanctuary: wood, canopy green, marble, water, dappled light, and warm forest calm.
- Make the homepage immediately bookable, but quieter and more specialist than urgent high-volume competitors.
- Feature prenatal care, but do not make the business feel only prenatal. Keep pathways for lymphatic flow, head/jaw/headache care, nervous-system reset, restorative massage, ritual care, seniors, caregivers, sensitive bodies, travel recovery, and chronic-pain pacing.
- Turn "light touch" into expertise. The copy should say that gentleness is a skilled strategy for bodies that brace, not a lesser massage.
- Use the Rose Session Map and Care Compass as the digital edge: guests do not need to know the perfect service name before asking for care.
- Preserve strong safety language around prenatal, lymphatic, surgery, swelling, medical red flags, scope of practice, privacy, and future AI-assisted summaries.
- Build trust with education: a 36-article care library, clear care standards, and practical aftercare.
- Keep the Rose Quartz meaning grounded: unconditional care, compassion, consent, warmth, and protection rather than heavy crystal-store language.
- Treat Rose's water and seven symbolism as a proprietary method: the Seven Waters intake.
