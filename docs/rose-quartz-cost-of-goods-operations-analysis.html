What it costs to open, operate, and protect the margin.

Immediate Takeaway

Rose should treat massage as a service-margin business, not a product-margin business. The core cost driver is paid provider time, followed by rent, marketing, laundry, payment processing, and admin. The safest launch path is a premium one-room studio with tight fixed costs, a high 90-minute utilization strategy, and membership revenue only after no-show and rollover rules are clear.

Margin rule: If Rose pays a therapist, direct session costs should usually stay under 50%-55% of service revenue. If Rose is owner-operated, the P&L must still reserve an owner compensation line so profit is not confused with unpaid labor.

Cost of Goods Sold: What Belongs in COGS

COGS Line Typical Range Rose Planning Target Why It Matters
Provider labor for performed services 35%-50% of service revenue Owner-operated: track as owner comp. Staffed: cap near 40%-45% before tips. Largest variable cost. A 50% commission on a $190 member 90-minute session leaves less room for rent and marketing.
Backbar products: cream, oil, gel, aromatics, hot towel supplies 4%-6% of service sales in salon/spa benchmarks $3-$8 per session unless premium aromatics or stones are added. Low per-session cost, but waste, overuse, and too many SKUs can quietly erode margin.
Linen and laundry $6-$14 per session if outsourced at 4-7 lb/session Budget $8 per service at launch; renegotiate after volume is known. Clean linens are a safety cost, not an optional amenity. Oil-heavy linens also need a fire-safe laundry process.
Payment processing About 2.6%-3.5% plus fixed fee depending on card-present or card-on-file Use 3.2% in models. A $220 card payment is roughly $6-$8. Memberships, deposits, and card-on-file transactions can cost more than tap/dip/swipe payments.
Retail inventory cost 48%-54% of retail revenue Only add retail after service operations are stable. Retail can help margin, but only if inventory turns and staff can sell without making the brand feel pushy.

Benchmark anchor: salon/spa COGS often include service provider wages, backbar products, retail product costs, and credit card fees; the cited benchmark range is 45%-55% of total revenue, with professional product budgets around 4%-6% of service sales.

Typical Margins and Markups to Compare Against

Service gross margin

Healthy service gross margin after direct session labor, supplies, laundry, and card fees should land near 45%-55% for a staffed spa model. If Rose is owner-operated, the margin can look much higher, but that is because owner labor has not been paid yet.

Net profit / EBITDA

A well-managed spa commonly targets 10%-20% net profit. A young studio may run below that while buying reviews, refining occupancy, and absorbing startup inefficiency.

Retail markup

If retail product cost is 48%-54% of retail revenue, the implied markup on cost is roughly 85%-108%. Example: a product bought for $13 should retail around $24-$27 before discounts.

Backbar markup

Backbar is not normally marked up directly unless sold as an enhancement. If an aroma, hot towel, stone, or ritual add-on uses extra setup and supplies, price it from labor/time plus cost, not product cost alone.

Items Founders Often Forget

  • Lease clause expressly allowing massage therapy and the exact operating model.
  • City vs. Clark County jurisdiction check before signing anything.
  • Special Use Permit, zoning, proximity, parking, signage, and ADA path review.
  • Background and financial suitability timelines before opening date is promised.
  • Owner or approved key employee coverage during all operating hours.
  • Posted rates, posted licenses, ad license numbers, and front-desk compliance binder.
  • Health card, CPR, continuing education, and license renewal calendar.
  • Workers compensation, payroll taxes, overtime, and employee vs. contractor classification.
  • Membership rollover, cancellation, refund, dormancy, gift-card liability, and chargeback policy.
  • Client intake privacy, secure forms, data retention, and breach response.
  • No-show deposits and card-on-file language before online booking goes live.
  • Oil-stained linen fire risk, dryer vent cleaning, and laundry SOP.
  • Pregnancy contraindication and provider-guidance language.
  • Fragrance sensitivity, nut oils, CBD, stones, cupping, and modality insurance exclusions.
  • Music licensing if commercial music is played outside a licensed platform.
  • Review-generation system after every completed appointment.
  • Replacement reserve for linens, bolsters, table vinyl, face cradles, and small tools.
  • Retail sales tax, inventory shrink, expired products, and return policy.
  • Cleaning labor between guests, not just the visible treatment time.
  • Emergency plan: client fainting, prenatal issue, boundary violation, harassment, or injury.

Decision Checklist Before Rose Commits to a Lease

  • Confirm jurisdiction: City of Las Vegas, Clark County, Henderson, or North Las Vegas.
  • Confirm massage use is permitted in writing.
  • Ask licensing department whether Special Use Permit is required for the exact address.
  • Request landlord TI allowance, rent abatement, and permission for signage.
  • Model rent at 4%, 8%, 12%, and 15% of expected monthly revenue.
  • Confirm water, laundry, electrical, HVAC, sound, ADA, restroom, and parking constraints.
  • Build a 90-day opening timeline with licensing buffers.
  • Price insurance with landlord additional insured requirements before signing.
  • Decide if Rose is owner-operated only or will hire within first 12 months.
  • Decide whether membership starts at opening or after utilization stabilizes.
  • Set a minimum cash reserve that covers 3 months of fixed expense plus owner draw.
  • Do not add costly services such as facials, cupping, CBD, retail, or events until the core massage margin works.
